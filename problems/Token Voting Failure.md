> [!warning] Critical Urgency — Score: 70
> Raised by 22/30 participants · Average 4.2 messages per discussion · 13 solutions proposed

The most widely reported governance failure across the entire research program. Token-weighted voting concentrates decision-making power among large holders (whales), creating de facto plutocracy. Small holders are systematically marginalized regardless of their expertise or stake in outcomes. Nearly three-quarters of all interviewees raised this as a fundamental structural flaw in DAO governance.

## Evidence

**Breadth**: 22 out of 30 interviewees raised this problem, making it the single most widely recognized governance failure in the dataset. Its near-universal prevalence suggests this is not an edge case but a systemic design flaw baked into the dominant governance paradigm.

**Depth**: Averaging 4.2 messages per discussion, participants engaged substantively with this problem — not just flagging it but exploring its mechanics, consequences, and potential remedies. The combination of extreme breadth and solid depth produced the highest composite urgency score in the study.

## Why It Matters

Token voting is the default governance mechanism for most DAOs and protocols. Its failure cascades through every other governance function — from treasury allocation to protocol upgrades. Without addressing this, other governance improvements operate on a broken foundation. When the base layer of decision-making is structurally captured by capital concentration, no amount of process improvement on top can produce legitimately democratic outcomes.

## Proposed Solutions

- [[Conviction Voting]] — Replaces discrete votes with continuous signal, reducing the advantage of large one-time token holders
- [[Soulbound Reputation Tokens]] — Non-transferable tokens that weight governance power by participation and expertise rather than capital
- [[Delegate Verification]] — Ensures delegates represent genuine constituencies rather than whale interests
- [[AI Governance Agents]] — Automated analysis to surface when voting outcomes diverge from broader community sentiment
- [[Polis Opinion Clustering]] — Identifies genuine opinion clusters across the community, bypassing token-weighted distortions
- [[Signals Protocol]] — Allows nuanced preference expression beyond binary yes/no voting
- [[Co-creation Cycles]] — Shifts governance from voting on finished proposals to collaborative development, reducing the impact of vote-stage capture
- [[Proto-DAOs]] — Small-scale governance experiments that can test alternatives to token voting before full deployment
- [[VE Buyback Models]] — Vote-escrowed tokenomics that align long-term commitment with governance power
- [[VERP Protocol]] — Verified engagement and reputation protocol that diversifies governance weight beyond token holdings
- [[Pairwise Preference Voting]] — Replaces full-slate voting with simple binary comparisons, reducing the decisiveness of capital concentration
- [[Updraft]] — Proposal filtering system that surfaces quality proposals regardless of proposer's token holdings
- [[Token-as-Equity]] — Reframes token governance as shareholder governance with corresponding legal obligations and protections

## Related Problems

- [[Governance Theater]] — 15 participants raised both problems, suggesting that plutocratic voting outcomes are a primary driver of governance becoming performative. When whales control outcomes, governance processes become legitimacy theater for predetermined decisions.
- [[Voting Fatigue]] — 12 participants raised both problems, indicating that the futility of voting against whales directly contributes to voter disengagement and apathy.
- [[Informal Power]] — 9 participants raised both problems, revealing how token concentration enables narrative capture. Whales wield influence not just through votes but through the social dynamics surrounding governance.
