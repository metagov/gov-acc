Power in DAOs often flows through informal channels — social media influence, backchannel conversations, and narrative control — rather than through formal governance processes. Those who control the narrative effectively control governance outcomes regardless of formal mechanisms. This problem is insidious precisely because it operates outside the systems designed to create transparency and accountability.

## Evidence

**Breadth**: 17 out of 49 interviewees raised this problem, placing it in the upper-middle range of prevalence. The participants who raised it tended to be those with extensive DAO experience who had observed the gap between formal governance structures and actual decision-making dynamics firsthand.

**Depth**: Averaging 3.0 messages per discussion, participants engaged meaningfully with the mechanics of informal power — how it accumulates, how it is exercised, and why formal governance structures fail to constrain it. The moderate depth reflects a problem that is recognizable but nuanced in its manifestations.

## Why It Matters

Informal power is invisible to most governance frameworks and tools. On-chain voting records, proposal histories, and treasury flows create an appearance of transparency while the real influence networks that shape these outcomes remain opaque. This makes it impossible to evaluate whether governance outcomes reflect genuine community preferences or manufactured consensus. Without addressing informal power, even perfect formal mechanisms will produce captured outcomes.

## Proposed Solutions

- [[Conviction Voting]] — Continuous signaling makes it harder for narrative campaigns to create sudden vote swings around discrete decisions
- [[Soulbound Reputation Tokens]] — Makes governance influence legible and tied to verifiable participation rather than social capital
- [[Delegate Verification]] — Ensures that those wielding delegated power have transparent track records and genuine constituency support
- [[Polis Opinion Clustering]] — Surfaces genuine opinion distributions across the community, making narrative capture visible by contrasting manufactured consensus with actual sentiment
- [[Reference Identifiers]] — Creates persistent, verifiable identity markers that make influence networks traceable across governance contexts
- [[Telescope Bot]] — Automated monitoring of governance discussions to surface patterns of narrative coordination and informal influence
- [[Tensegrity Governance]] — Structural governance design that distributes authority across interdependent roles, making it harder for informal power to concentrate
- [[Updraft]] — Community curation of proposals reduces the effectiveness of narrative campaigns by creating multiple evaluation touchpoints
- [[Explicit Interest Group Representation]] — Formal interest groups within protocols make power dynamics explicit and visible, reducing the advantage of informal influence networks

## Related Problems

- [[Token Voting Failure]] — 9 participants raised both problems, revealing how token concentration amplifies informal power. Whales wield influence not just through their votes but through the social dynamics, media presence, and backchannel conversations their economic position enables.
- [[Governance Theater]] — 8 participants raised both problems, highlighting the symbiotic relationship between informal power and theatrical governance. When real decisions happen informally, formal governance becomes theater by definition.
- [[Voting Fatigue]] — 6 participants raised both problems, suggesting that awareness of informal power dynamics contributes to voter disengagement. Participants who perceive governance as informally captured see little point in formal participation.
