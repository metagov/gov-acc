A framework for fair dispute resolution through DAO-vetted, subject-matter-specific juror pools. Instead of pure economic staking (where wealthy participants dominate) or Schelling Point voting (where jurors vote with the majority rather than for what's right), this approach establishes qualification standards and community-refreshed pools of competent jurors.

## How It Works

Potential jurors apply and are vetted by a DAO that sets standards for specific subject-matter courts. The qualified pool is refreshed via community voting to reflect current values and evolving expertise needs. Cases are assigned from this pool based on subject-matter expertise, with random selection within the qualified set to prevent manipulation.

Soulbound Tokens or non-transferable reputation tokens determine juror weight based on participation history and ruling fairness, not economic stake. This creates a fundamentally different incentive structure from existing decentralized arbitration systems: jurors are rewarded for demonstrating competence over time rather than for staking capital or predicting majority opinion. The constitutional framing — where the DAO defines and periodically revises juror qualification criteria — provides democratic legitimacy that pure algorithmic selection lacks.

## Maturity Assessment

At Genesis stage (ex 1.5), constitutional juror pools remain largely conceptual. Kleros v2.0 is implementing modular dispute kits that incorporate some elements of expertise-based filtering, but the full model — with DAO-governed qualification standards, community-refreshed pools, and SBT-weighted deliberation — has not been deployed as a complete system. The theoretical foundations are being developed in parallel at IIT-Madras, suggesting this solution may mature as its component parts converge.
