Systems for verifying delegate identity, track record, and active engagement. Delegate verification creates accountability infrastructure that enables consistent compensation, transparent performance tracking, and informed delegation choices. It addresses the gap between the promise of representative governance in DAOs and the reality of opaque, unaccountable delegation.

## How It Works

Delegate verification operates across multiple dimensions. At the most basic level, it confirms that a delegate is a real, unique person — not a sybil or sock puppet. Beyond identity, verification systems track on-chain voting records, proposal authorship, forum participation, and committee attendance to build a comprehensive delegate profile.

More advanced implementations create delegate scorecards that aggregate multiple metrics: voting participation rate, alignment between stated positions and actual votes, responsiveness to delegator communications, and domain expertise indicators. These scorecards serve as the basis for informed delegation decisions and, increasingly, for compensation.

The compensation angle is critical. Many DAOs struggle with "delegate sustainability" — the problem that governance participation is time-consuming but unpaid. Verification systems enable performance-based delegate compensation by providing objective metrics against which to measure contribution. This transforms delegation from a volunteer activity into a sustainable role.

Some systems also include credential verification, confirming relevant expertise claims (e.g., a delegate claiming security audit experience actually has verifiable audit work). This helps delegators match their tokens to delegates with relevant domain knowledge.

## Problems Addressed

- [[Token Voting Failure]] — Improves the quality of delegated voting by making delegate competence and engagement visible, reducing the chance that governance power pools in the hands of disengaged or unqualified delegates
- [[Delegate Sustainability]] — Enables performance-based compensation by providing objective measures of delegate contribution, creating sustainable incentives for high-quality governance participation
- [[Informal Power]] — Makes delegation relationships transparent and trackable, reducing the influence of behind-the-scenes coordination and making governance power flows legible

## Key Actors

- **Scroll DAO** — Implementing delegate verification as part of their broader governance framework, tying verification to compensation eligibility
- **Forward** — Building delegate reputation and verification infrastructure for cross-DAO use
- **DeepDAO** — Providing analytics and tracking for delegate activity across multiple DAOs, enabling cross-ecosystem reputation visibility

## Maturity Assessment

At the Custom-to-Product boundary (ex 3), delegate verification is being actively implemented by several organizations but hasn't yet standardized. The core challenge is balancing comprehensiveness with privacy — delegates need accountability, but overly invasive verification can deter participation. Cross-DAO portability of delegate credentials remains an open design question. The convergence of multiple teams on similar approaches suggests an emerging consensus on the basic architecture.
