Simulated jury systems that interpret programmatic ethics and resolve disputes using AI-mediated processes. AI dispute resolution moves beyond pure mechanism design by incorporating contextual judgment into governance conflicts — recognizing that not all disputes can be resolved through voting or economic incentives alone.

## How It Works

Traditional on-chain dispute resolution (like Kleros or Aragon Court) relies on human jurors who are economically incentivized to vote with the majority. AI dispute resolution takes a different approach: it uses AI models to analyze disputes in context, considering relevant precedent, community norms, and the specific circumstances of the case.

The "simulated jury" model works by training or prompting AI systems on a community's governance documents, past decisions, and stated values. When a dispute arises, the AI generates a preliminary judgment along with its reasoning, which can then be reviewed and ratified (or overturned) by human participants. This inverts the typical model — instead of humans doing the analysis and AI providing tools, the AI does initial analysis and humans provide oversight.

More ambitiously, some approaches envision AI systems that can interpret the intent behind smart contracts when edge cases arise. Smart contracts are notoriously rigid — they execute exactly what was coded, not what was intended. AI dispute resolution could serve as an interpretive layer that mediates between code and intent, particularly in cases where programmatic execution produces outcomes that violate the community's understood norms.

## Maturity Assessment

At Genesis-to-Custom (ex 2), AI dispute resolution is still largely conceptual with early prototypes. The technical capabilities are advancing rapidly — large language models can increasingly parse complex governance documents and reason about norms — but the trust infrastructure is not yet in place. Communities are understandably cautious about delegating dispute resolution to AI systems, and questions about bias, transparency, and appeal mechanisms remain unresolved. This solution likely needs several more iterations before reaching Product stage.
