Gated governance models with progressive decentralization. Proto-DAOs allow organizations to prove product-market fit and governance capacity before fully decentralizing, using staked governance to ensure participants have skin in the game. This challenges the orthodoxy that organizations should launch as fully decentralized DAOs from day one.

## How It Works

A Proto-DAO starts as a more centralized structure — perhaps a foundation, a core team, or a small multisig — and progressively transfers governance power to its community as both the product and the governance capacity mature. The key insight is that premature decentralization often produces worse outcomes than gradual transition, because effective governance requires institutional knowledge, established norms, and a sufficiently engaged community.

The progressive decentralization typically follows a staged model. In the early stages, the founding team retains most decision-making authority while building community and establishing governance norms. As the community demonstrates capacity — through participation in advisory votes, committee work, and proposal feedback — real governance power is transferred. The final stage sees full community governance, with the founding team becoming participants rather than controllers.

Staked governance is a common feature: participants must stake tokens to participate in governance decisions, ensuring that they have material commitment to the organization's outcomes. This addresses the drive-by voting problem where participants with no real stake in the organization influence its direction.

Some Proto-DAO models include explicit maturity criteria for each decentralization stage: minimum participation rates, demonstrated governance competence (e.g., successful management of a committee budget), and community size thresholds. This prevents both premature decentralization (where governance is transferred before the community is ready) and perpetual centralization (where the founding team never actually gives up control).

## Problems Addressed

- [[Lack of Clear Purpose]] — The staged approach forces organizations to articulate their purpose and governance goals at each stage rather than launching with vague decentralization promises
- [[Token Voting Failure]] — Staked governance ensures that governance participants have material commitment, and progressive decentralization means token voting is only introduced when the community has the capacity to use it effectively
- [[Governance Theater]] — By honestly acknowledging that early-stage organizations need centralized decision-making, Proto-DAOs avoid the pretense of decentralized governance that masks founder control

## Maturity Assessment

At Genesis-to-Custom (ex 2), the Proto-DAO concept is well-articulated but implementation frameworks are still developing. The idea of progressive decentralization is widely accepted in principle, but few organizations have formalized the process with explicit stage gates and maturity criteria. The primary tension is political: DAO communities often demand immediate decentralization, and founding teams that propose staged approaches face accusations of maintaining illegitimate control.
