> [!abstract] Financial · Product
> 5 participant mentions · Addresses 2 governance problems

Tiered compensation systems that create sustainable career paths within DAOs: Bounties for one-off tasks, Project Grants for defined deliverables, and Core Contributor Streams with vested tokens for ongoing work. Contributor Streams address the fundamental sustainability problem — DAOs need reliable contributors but offer no career stability.

## How It Works

Contributor Streams create a compensation ladder with increasing commitment and reward at each tier:

**Tier 1: Bounties.** Small, well-defined tasks with immediate payment upon completion. Bounties serve as an entry point for new contributors, allowing them to demonstrate capability and build reputation without long-term commitment. Examples include bug fixes, documentation updates, or small feature implementations.

**Tier 2: Project Grants.** Larger, milestone-based funding for defined projects. Contributors submit proposals, receive funding tied to deliverable milestones, and are evaluated on outcomes. Grants bridge the gap between bounties and ongoing roles, enabling medium-term focused work.

**Tier 3: Core Contributor Streams.** Recurring compensation (often monthly) with vested tokens for contributors who take on ongoing, critical functions. Streams include a base salary component (often in stablecoins for reliability) plus a token vesting component that aligns long-term incentives with the organization's success.

The token vesting component is crucial. Core contributors receive governance tokens that vest over 1-2 years, ensuring they remain aligned with the organization's long-term health. This mirrors the equity-based compensation model from traditional startups, adapted for decentralized contexts.

Some implementations include performance reviews — either by committee evaluation, peer assessment, or outcome-based metrics — that determine stream continuation and potential advancement. This creates accountability without the managerial hierarchy of traditional organizations.

## Problems Addressed

- [[Broken Contributor Economies]] — Directly addresses the lack of sustainable compensation models in DAOs by creating clear career paths with increasing commitment and reward
- [[Delegate Sustainability]] — Core Contributor Streams can include governance participation as an explicit responsibility, creating paid roles for active governance contributors

## Key Actors

- **Scroll DAO** — Implementing a tiered contributor compensation system with clear paths from bounties through core contributor streams, including token vesting for long-term alignment
- **Various DAOs** — Multiple organizations are developing contributor stream models, contributing to an emerging set of best practices for decentralized compensation

## Maturity Assessment

At Product stage (ex 3.5), Contributor Streams are being actively implemented by many major DAOs. The tiered model is well-understood and has proven effective at attracting and retaining talent. However, important design questions remain: how to set fair compensation levels across geographies, how to handle contributor underperformance without managerial authority, and how to balance stablecoin and token compensation given volatile crypto markets. The pattern is approaching commodity status for the basic bounty-and-grants layers but retains Product-stage complexity for core contributor streams.

## Participant Mentions

Referenced in 5 out of 30 interviews. The high mention count reflects the universality of the contributor sustainability problem. Nearly every DAO struggles to attract and retain talent, and Contributor Streams represents the most developed answer. Participants who mentioned this solution often emphasized the importance of the vesting component — without long-term incentive alignment, contributors optimize for short-term extraction rather than organizational health.
