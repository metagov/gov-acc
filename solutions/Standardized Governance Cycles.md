Scheduled voting windows and quarterly funding slate votes that replace the always-on, proposal-by-proposal governance model. Standardized Governance Cycles give participants predictable cadences for engagement, reducing the cognitive load of constant governance monitoring and allowing focused, high-quality participation during defined periods.

## How It Works

Instead of continuous proposal submission and voting — where any participant can post a proposal at any time and voters must be perpetually attentive — Standardized Governance Cycles create fixed windows for governance activity. The most common implementation is quarterly governance cycles where proposals are batched, reviewed, and voted on as a slate during a defined voting window.

In a quarterly funding slate model, project funding requests are collected over the quarter and presented as competing funding slates. Token holders vote once per quarter, choosing among curated slates rather than evaluating individual proposals. This dramatically reduces the number of decisions voters must make while increasing the quality of attention given to each decision.

The cycle approach has precedent in traditional corporate governance (annual general meetings, quarterly board sessions) and legislative processes (legislative sessions with defined calendars). DAOs that have implemented governance cycles report that participation rates stabilize and the quality of deliberation improves when participants can plan their governance engagement around predictable schedules.

## Problems Addressed

- [[Voting Fatigue]] — Directly reduces the volume of governance decisions by batching proposals into predictable cycles, allowing participants to concentrate their attention rather than spreading it across continuous voting events
- [[Token Voting Failure]] — Slate-based voting reduces the frequency at which token weight determines individual outcomes, and batch deliberation creates space for coalition-building that can counterbalance whale dominance

## Key Actors

- **Loring** — Proposed the concept based on observations that many DAOs are already moving toward standardized governance cycles as a response to voter fatigue, citing the cypherpunk DAO model of quarterly funding slate votes
- **Karam (pre-flight packets)** — Advocating for structured pre-flight documentation packets that standardize the preparation phase of governance cycles, ensuring proposals meet quality thresholds before entering the voting pipeline

## Maturity Assessment

At Custom stage (ex 2.5), several DAOs have implemented governance cycles informally or semi-formally, but the pattern has not yet been standardized into reusable tooling. The concept draws on well-established practices from corporate and legislative governance. Moving to Product stage requires developing tooling that supports cycle-based governance natively, including proposal batching, slate creation, and cycle-aware notification systems.
