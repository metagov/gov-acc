Collective sensemaking through opinion mapping that identifies natural consensus clusters and bridges between divided groups. Polis is one of the most mature deliberation tools available, with a proven track record in civic governance contexts extending well beyond web3. Its algorithmic approach to finding common ground makes it a uniquely powerful pre-governance tool.

## How It Works

Polis presents participants with a series of statements that they can agree with, disagree with, or pass on. As responses accumulate, the system uses dimensionality reduction (specifically, principal component analysis) to map participants into opinion clusters — groups of people who tend to agree with each other. Crucially, Polis does not ask participants to respond to each other directly. This eliminates the dynamics of debate and persuasion, instead surfacing the actual opinion landscape.

The most powerful output of a Polis conversation is not the majority opinion but the "bridging statements" — positions that find support across otherwise divided clusters. These bridging statements represent genuine consensus that might never emerge through traditional debate, where participants tend to emphasize their differences rather than their commonalities.

In a governance context, Polis can serve as a pre-proposal sensemaking layer. Before drafting a formal proposal, a governance facilitator runs a Polis conversation to understand where the community actually stands. The resulting opinion map reveals which aspects of an issue have natural consensus and which are genuinely contentious, enabling more targeted and effective proposal design.

## Problems Addressed

- [[Token Voting Failure]] — Provides a sensemaking layer that surfaces genuine community preferences before they are distorted by token-weighted voting, enabling governance designs that reflect actual consensus
- [[Informal Power]] — Democratizes agenda-setting by making community opinion visible without requiring participants to navigate social hierarchies or speak up in public forums
- [[Voting Fatigue]] — Reduces unnecessary votes by identifying areas of natural consensus that don't need formal governance processes, focusing voting attention where genuine disagreement exists

## Key Actors

- **Polis** — The original platform, developed by Colin Megill and team, used in Taiwan's vTaiwan process, multiple civic engagement projects, and increasingly in web3 governance contexts
- **Metagov** — Researching and promoting the use of sensemaking tools including Polis for decentralized governance, with particular attention to integration with existing DAO toolchains

## Maturity Assessment

At Commodity stage (ex 4), Polis is the most mature sensemaking tool in the governance solutions landscape. It has been battle-tested in high-stakes civic contexts (Taiwan's national policy deliberations, multiple municipal governments) and has a substantial body of research validating its effectiveness. The technology is open source and well-documented. The primary adoption barrier in the web3 context is not the tool itself but the cultural shift required — moving from "vote on everything" to "sense-make first, then vote on what matters."

## Participant Mentions

Referenced in 5 out of 40 interviews. The high mention count, combined with Commodity maturity, confirms Polis's status as a known and respected tool. Participants frequently referenced it as an example of what effective governance tooling looks like — and as evidence that the sensemaking layer of governance is underdeveloped relative to the voting layer. Several noted that Polis works well for surfacing opinions but doesn't solve the harder problem of integrating sensemaking outputs into binding governance decisions.
