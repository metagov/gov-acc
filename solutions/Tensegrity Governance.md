> [!abstract] Structural · Genesis
> 3 participant mentions · Addresses 4 governance problems

A governance framework inspired by the structural engineering concept of tensegrity — holding paradoxes in dynamic tension rather than resolving them. Tensegrity governance creates multiple decision venues and constitutional space for contradictions, moving beyond binary voting models toward a more organic approach to organizational coherence.

## How It Works

In structural engineering, tensegrity (tensional integrity) describes structures that maintain their shape through a balance of compression and tension elements — think of a suspension bridge or a Buckminster Fuller sculpture. No single element bears all the load; the structure's integrity emerges from the dynamic balance between competing forces.

Tensegrity governance applies this principle to organizational design. Rather than trying to resolve all tensions through voting (which produces winners and losers), it creates a governance architecture where different values, priorities, and constituencies hold each other in productive tension. Multiple decision venues coexist, each optimized for different types of decisions and different stakeholder groups.

For example, a tensegrity governance system might have separate but interconnected decision spaces for technical protocol decisions, community resource allocation, and strategic direction — each with different participation rules, different decision mechanisms, and different legitimacy sources. The "tension" between these spaces (e.g., technical priorities vs. community preferences vs. strategic goals) is not a bug to be fixed but a feature that produces more resilient outcomes.

The framework also creates explicit "constitutional space" for paradoxes — places where contradictory principles are acknowledged and held rather than prematurely resolved. A DAO might simultaneously value decentralization and operational efficiency, for instance. Rather than pretending one must win, tensegrity governance designs structures that maintain both in productive tension.

## Problems Addressed

- [[Over-Reliance on Game Theory]] — Offers a fundamentally different paradigm for governance design that draws from structural engineering and systems thinking rather than economics
- [[Governance Theater]] — Creates multiple meaningful decision venues rather than funneling everything through a single voting mechanism that may not be appropriate for all decision types
- [[Lack of Clear Purpose]] — The constitutional design process forces organizations to articulate their core values and tensions explicitly, creating clarity about what the organization is trying to hold together
- [[Informal Power]] — Multiple decision venues with different participation rules reduce the ability of any single faction to dominate across all governance contexts

## Key Actors

- **Durgadas** — The primary architect of the tensegrity governance framework, drawing on deep study of structural engineering principles, indigenous governance systems, and organizational design theory

## Maturity Assessment

At Genesis stage (ex 1.5), tensegrity governance is the most theoretically ambitious framework in the solutions landscape. Its strength is its philosophical depth — it offers a genuinely different way of thinking about governance that goes beyond mechanism tweaks. Its weakness is implementation complexity: translating the elegant metaphor of tensegrity into practical governance structures requires significant design expertise, and no large-scale implementations yet exist. The framework may be most valuable as a design philosophy that influences more specific solutions rather than as a standalone governance system.

## Participant Mentions

Referenced in 3 out of 40 interviews. For a Genesis-stage solution, 3 mentions is relatively high, suggesting that the concept resonates with practitioners who have experienced the limitations of binary voting models. Participants who mentioned tensegrity governance were typically those most frustrated with the reductionism of "just vote on it" approaches and most interested in governance designs that can hold complexity without collapsing it.
