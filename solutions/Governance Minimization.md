An honesty-by-design approach that makes explicit what a DAO actually controls versus what it does not, reducing the surface area for governance theater by limiting governance to domains where it can genuinely function. Rather than expanding governance to cover everything, governance minimization narrows it to what matters — and is honest about the rest.

## How It Works

Many DAOs suffer from a mismatch between what they claim to govern and what they actually govern. Formal voting processes are applied to decisions where they have no real enforcement power, or where the outcome is effectively predetermined by core teams or foundations. Governance minimization addresses this by:

1. **Auditing the actual scope of governance** — Mapping which decisions the DAO genuinely controls versus which are controlled by foundations, core teams, multisigs, or market forces
2. **Being explicit about the boundaries** — Rather than pretending the DAO governs everything, clearly documenting what falls within and outside community governance authority
3. **Focusing governance energy** — Directing participation, deliberation, and voting toward decisions where governance actually has power, reducing fatigue from engaging with theatrical processes

This approach is related to but distinct from [[Optimistic Governance]] (which changes the default from "vote on everything" to "object when needed"). Governance minimization is more fundamental — it asks whether the governance domain itself should be narrower, not just whether individual decisions should have a different default.

The concept also connects to the combined approach Bianca describes in her academic work: prevention (design systems to avoid disputes) + mediation (human dispute resolution) + on-chain enforcement (where code can handle it) + hybrid off-chain resolution (where it can't). Each layer is honest about what it can and cannot do.

## Problems Addressed

- [[Governance Theater]] — Directly attacks the root cause of theater by making explicit the gap between formal governance scope and actual decision-making power, rather than maintaining the pretense
- [[Technical and Legal Gaps]] — By being honest about what onchain governance can and cannot enforce, reduces the expectation gap that creates legal and technical vulnerabilities

## Maturity Assessment

At Genesis-to-Custom (ex 2), governance minimization is primarily a framing and design principle rather than a specific tool or protocol. Some elements are already practiced informally — protocols like Lido have been explicit about what their DAO does and doesn't control ([[Specialized Committees|dual governance model]]). But as a systematic methodology for scoping governance, it remains early-stage. The main challenge is that being honest about governance limitations requires the kind of transparency that governance theater exists to avoid.
